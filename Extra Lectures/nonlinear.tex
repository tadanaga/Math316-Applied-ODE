\section{A Spiral and a Saddle}

\subsection{From Local Behavior to a Phase Portrait}

Before studying another population model, let's review how to put
local sketches together. The following system is a mathematical review
example; its coordinates are not populations, so negative coordinates
are allowed. A nonlinear system may have several equilibria, each with
a different local behavior.

To sketch its phase portrait:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Find all equilibria.
    \item Compute the Jacobian.
    \item Classify each equilibrium using the
    eigenvalues of the Jacobian.
    \item Sketch the local behavior.
    \item Use the original equations to guide
    trajectories farther from the equilibria.
\end{enumerate}

When all eigenvalues have nonzero real parts,
linearization determines the local qualitative
behavior. However, it does not determine the
entire phase portrait.

%-------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Finding and Classifying the Equilibria}

\begin{example}[1]{
Find and classify the equilibria, then sketch the
local behavior of
\[
\begin{dcases}
x'=1-x-y,\\
y'=x^2-1.
\end{dcases}
\]
}

\paragraph{Step 1. Find the equilibria.}
Set both components equal to zero:
\[
1-x-y=0,\qquad x^2-1=0.
\]
The second equation gives $x=\pm1$.

If $x=1$, then $y=0$.
If $x=-1$, then $y=2$.
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{(1,0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(-1,2).}
\]

\paragraph{Step 2. Compute the Jacobian.}
Let
\[
f(x,y)=1-x-y,\qquad g(x,y)=x^2-1.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
J(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-1\\
2x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

\paragraph{Step 3. Classify $(1,0)$ and orient the spiral.}
At this equilibrium,
\[
A_1=J(1,0)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(\lambda I-A_1)=\lambda^2+\lambda+2.
\]
Thus $\lambda=(-1\pm i\sqrt7)/2$. The real part is negative, so
$(1,0)$ is a \textbf{stable spiral}.

To orient it, test a point just to the right: $(1+\varepsilon,0)$,
where $\varepsilon>0$. The original equations give
\[
(x',y')=(-\varepsilon,\,2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2).
\]
The velocity points up and left, so the spiral rotates counterclockwise.

\paragraph{Step 4. Classify $(-1,2)$ and find its directions.}
Here,
\[
A_2=J(-1,2)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(\lambda I-A_2)=\lambda^2+\lambda-2
=(\lambda-1)(\lambda+2).
\]
The eigenvalues have opposite signs, so $(-1,2)$ is a \textbf{saddle}.
Solving $(A_2-\lambda I)\mathbf v=\mathbf0$ gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\lambda & \text{Eigenvector} & \text{Tangent line through }(-1,2)\\\hline
-2 & (1,1)^T & y-2=x+1\quad\text{(inward)}\\
1 & (1,-2)^T & y-2=-2(x+1)\quad\text{(outward)}
\end{array}
\]
For example, when $\lambda=1$, the first row gives
$-2v_1-v_2=0$, so we may choose $(v_1,v_2)=(1,-2)$.
Translate each direction to the equilibrium before drawing its arrows.
\end{example}

\subsection{Sketching the Local Behavior}

Plot the equilibria and draw:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a counterclockwise inward spiral near $(1,0)$;
    \item a saddle near $(-1,2)$, with inward arrows
    along $(1,1)$ and outward arrows along $(1,-2)$.
\end{itemize}

The saddle lines indicate tangent directions.
The nonlinear trajectories need not remain straight.

\subsection{Extending the Sketch}

To guide the trajectories farther away, return
to the original equations:
\[
x'=1-x-y,\qquad y'=x^2-1.
\]
They give the following directions:
\[
\begin{aligned}
y<1-x&\implies x'>0,
&
y>1-x&\implies x'<0,\\
|x|>1&\implies y'>0,
&
|x|<1&\implies y'<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The curves where one derivative vanishes are called
\textbf{nullclines}. Here they are
\[
y=1-x,\qquad x=-1,\qquad x=1.
\]

Use these signs together with the local behavior
to sketch trajectories. Do not extend the saddle
eigendirections as straight lines across the
entire plane.
